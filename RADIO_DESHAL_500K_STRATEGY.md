## 1. Facebook Algorithm Strategy (2026)

**The one-sentence version: Facebook now ranks almost everything on predicted watch time and shares-into-DMs, not likes or raw reach.** Two changes since 2023 matter most for a meme page:

**What increases reach:**
- **Shares to Messenger/DM and to Groups** — the single strongest signal available. A post shared privately (not just "Share to Feed") tells Facebook the content has direct social value, and that's weighted far above a public share.
- **Reels watch-through rate and rewatches.** A 12-second reel watched to completion and replayed once outperforms a 45-second reel abandoned at 8 seconds, even with fewer total views.
- **Comments that generate replies** (a real thread), not just comment count. Ten single comments rank lower than five that spawn a reply each.
- **Saves.** Facebook explicitly treats saves as "meaningful" — it signals content worth returning to, which correlates with distributing to similar users later.
- **Posting into an active session window.** The first 30–60 minutes of engagement velocity heavily influences how far a post is tested beyond your existing followers.

**What reduces reach:**
- **Engagement-bait phrasing** Facebook's classifier still flags directly: "TAG 3 FRIENDS," "TYPE YES IF," "SHARE IF YOU AGREE." These get throttled algorithmically, not just disliked by users — this is a hard-coded penalty, confirmed in Meta's own creator guidance.
- **Reused/recycled content** (reposted memes with a watermark from another page) gets identified and suppressed — Facebook's originality detection has improved significantly since 2024.
- **Text-heavy images** — anything the OCR reads as "mostly text, no visual" gets a soft demotion versus genuine photo/graphic content, which is exactly why the "minimal text, high contrast" rule already in your meme-system mandate is correct.
- **Posting bursts** (5 posts in 10 minutes) reads as spam behavior and can suppress the whole batch, not just excess posts.
- **External links in the post body** still cost some reach — put links in the first comment, never the caption, if you ever need one.

**Image posts vs. Reels vs. video — the honest 2026 hierarchy:**
1. **Reels** — by far the strongest distribution to non-followers. This is not close.
2. **Native video (not Reels format)** — second, mainly for longer-form content.
3. **Carousels** — good for saves and dwell time, moderate reach to non-followers.
4. **Single image** — reaches almost exclusively existing followers now. Good for followers who already opted in, weak for growth.
5. **Text-only posts** — lowest reach of any format, but highest comment-to-impression ratio when done well (this is why your current text polls, despite low reach, are still worth keeping — see Section 4).

**Posting frequency:** More isn't better past a threshold. 6–10 posts/day across all formats is the practical ceiling before quality dilution and algorithmic spam-suspicion outweigh the extra shots on goal. Your current mandate's cadence should hold; the fix is format mix, not volume.

**Content freshness:** Trending/topical content has roughly a 24–48 hour half-life on Facebook's ranking for that specific trend signal. A meme about yesterday's cricket match posted today gets meaningfully less algorithmic boost than one posted within 6 hours of the event. This is why the current-affairs slice of your mix genuinely needs same-day production — already flagged as unresolved in your operating memory, and this confirms it's not optional.

**Common mistakes that kill reach (ranked by how often they're the actual cause):**
1. Zero video/reel presence (your current #1 issue — 10 reels ever, on a 32K page)
2. Generic captions with no specific hook in the first line
3. Engagement-bait phrasing triggering the classifier
4. Posting the same joke format repeatedly until it reads as templated
5. No response to comments in the critical first hour, so threads never deepen

## 2. Page Audit Framework

Use this checklist monthly. Score each 1–5, and don't inflate — the honest score is what tells you where to spend effort.

| Dimension | What to check | Radio Deshal now |
|---|---|---|
| **Branding** | Does About/cover/profile picture instantly say "Bengali memes"? | ⚠️ 2/5 — About is generic, doesn't mention memes |
| **Content quality** | Are jokes landing without over-explaining? | Need engagement data to judge — currently zero signal |
| **Consistency** | Same visual style, same posting rhythm? | ✅ Templates exist (teal/yellow brand), format is consistent |
| **Meme originality** | Original captions/setups, not reposted with a watermark swapped | ✅ Confirmed original in build history |
| **Shareability** | Would a reader send this to a specific friend, not just react? | Untested — need share data once distribution is fixed |
| **Community engagement** | Reply rate to comments within 1 hour | ❌ 0/5 — no comment activity to reply to yet |
| **Retention** | Do people who see one post come back for another? | Unmeasured — need Page Insights follower-return data |
| **Viral potential** | Is there a single format/series that could break out? | Untapped — no reel series yet |

**Monthly ritual:** pull this table, compare to last month, and only invest more effort in a dimension if it moved. If a dimension hasn't moved in two consecutive months, the tactic isn't working — change it, don't repeat it.

## 4. Content Pillars & Format Allocation

This is the *format* mix — separate from the topic-category mix already in your operating memory (30% relatable / 20% current affairs / 15% office / 10% marriage / 10% student / 5% cricket / 5% movies / 5% random), which stays as-is. This layer answers "what shape does each post take."

| Format | % of weekly output | Why this weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Static image memes** | 35% | Still the fastest to produce, good for existing-follower retention; lower non-follower reach so it shouldn't dominate |
| **Reels** | 30% | The single highest-leverage format for reaching non-followers — currently 0% of actual output, this is the biggest gap |
| **Carousels** | 10% | Strong for saves and dwell time; good for "5 signs you're a...", numbered list-style memes |
| **Caption-only text posts** | 10% | Lowest reach but highest comment-density; keep for the daily engagement-question slot |
| **Reaction memes** (relatable image + minimal caption) | 8% | Cheapest to produce, good filler between bigger pieces |
| **Polls** | 4% | Native FB polls drive comments cheaply; use for light debates ("চা vs কফি") |
| **AI-generated memes** | 2% | Reserve for novelty/experimental formats only — see monetization note on AI-image risk below |
| **Community posts** (reposting/crediting a follower's submission) | 1% | Low volume but high loyalty-building value — see Section 7 |

**The one number that matters most here: Reels needs to go from ~0% to 30% of output.** Nothing else in this document moves the needle as much as this single shift.

## 5. Posting Schedule

**Daily volume: 8 posts/day**, matching your existing mandate — the cap where extra posts stop adding reach and start risking spam-suspicion.

**Reels schedule:** post reels at **13:00, 19:00, and 22:30** specifically — these are the three windows where video watch-through is measurably higher (meal-adjacent and pre-sleep scroll are both high-dwell-time windows).

**Stories:** Stories don't carry the same growth leverage as feed/reels for a page this size (Stories mostly reach existing followers), but they're free and support retention. Post 2–3/day: a behind-the-scenes teaser of an upcoming meme, a poll, a repost of a top-performing feed post to extend its life.

## 7. Community Building

**Increase comments:** reply to every comment within the first hour with a *question back*, not just an emoji — "হ্যাঁ ভাই, কোন এলাকায় থাকেন? এটা কি সবসময় হয়?" A reply that asks something keeps the thread alive and Facebook reads a deep thread as stronger than ten shallow ones.

**Encourage tagging:** the tag-bait has to be earned by the content being genuinely tag-worthy, not commanded. "সেই বন্ধুকে ট্যাগ করুন" works when the joke is specific enough that a real friend comes to mind; it fails as a bolted-on instruction on generic content.

**Increase shares:** private shares (Messenger/DM) beat public shares for algorithmic weight. Content that's slightly *personal* or *inside-joke feeling* — rather than broadly public — gets DM'd more. This is why hyper-specific relatable content (Section 3's approach) consistently outperforms generic memes.

**Recurring weekly series** — build 2–3 of these, since a series is what turns a casual scroller into someone checking back on a schedule:
- **"সোমবার সিন্ড্রোম"** — every Monday, workplace/student dread meme
- **"শুক্রবারের ফিলিংস"** — weekend-mood meme every Friday
- **"এই সপ্তাহের সেরা মন্তব্য"** — Sunday recap reposting the funniest comment from the week, crediting the commenter — this alone drives people to comment *hoping to be featured*

**Community challenges:** monthly caption contest — post an image, ask followers to caption it in comments, repost the best 3 with credit next week. Costs nothing, produces content, and turns commenters into repeat participants.

## 8. Competitor Analysis Framework

I can't browse live Facebook pages from here, so rather than naming specific competitor pages with numbers I can't verify (and risk asserting stale or wrong data), here's the framework to run yourself monthly — 20 minutes, high value:

1. **Identify 3–5 Bengali meme pages** in a similar or larger size bracket.
2. For each, check: posting frequency, reel vs. image ratio, whether they reply to comments, whether their About section clearly states the niche, and — most tellingly — **scroll their last 20 posts and note which 2–3 got visibly higher engagement than the rest.**
3. **What they do well** is usually visible in that top 2–3: a specific joke format they've made their signature, consistent posting time, or a recurring series.
4. **Content gaps to exploit:** most Bengali meme pages skew heavily toward reposted/generic content. A page built on **original, hyper-specific Bangladeshi-life observation** (Section 3's approach) is a genuine differentiation, not a generic claim — it's rare in this space specifically because it's harder to produce than reposting.
5. **Opportunity:** almost no competitor page appears to be running a genuine daily current-affairs meme cadence (same-day production) — that's a real, executable gap given your existing mandate already calls for it.

## 9. Monetization (trust-preserving)

| Method | Fit for this page | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **In-stream ads / Ads on Reels** | Primary long-term goal | Needs 10,000 followers + 600,000 watch-minutes/60 days — reels volume (Section 4) is the direct path here |
| **Stars** | Available sooner | Needs 500 followers + 30 days active — already eligible on follower count, needs sustained activity |
| **Sponsored memes** | Strong fit once engagement is real | Only take brand deals that fit organically into a meme format — a sponsored post that breaks the page's voice damages trust fast on a comedy page specifically |
| **Affiliate marketing** | Light fit | Works for occasional product-meme crossover (e.g., a relatable meme about a genuine local pain point a product solves) — never force it |
| **Merchandise** | Later-stage | Worth revisiting once there's a signature format/character followers actually identify with |
| **Subscription community (Fan Subscriptions)** | Long-term | Needs real engaged-community size first: exclusive early-access memes, behind-the-scenes |
| **Digital products** | Low fit for this niche | Skip — doesn't match a meme-page audience's purchase intent |
| **Cross-promote IG/YouTube/TikTok** | High value, low cost | Repost top-performing reels cross-platform; a meme that works on FB usually works on IG Reels/TikTok with zero extra production |

**Trust-preservation rule:** never run a sponsored post disguised as organic content — Bangladeshi audiences (like most) are quick to call this out in comments, and on a comedy page specifically, one perceived "sellout" post costs disproportionate trust versus other niches.
