## 2. Audience Research

**Ideal audience:** adults making active day-to-day health decisions — not medical professionals, not people already deep in a fitness subculture. The person who reads a health tip on their lunch break and thinks "I should actually do that."

| Dimension | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Age groups** | Primary: 25–44 (peak health-decision-making years — career, family, first health scares). Secondary: 45–60 (preventive care becomes personally relevant) |
| **Countries** | English-language health content performs best in US, UK, Canada, Australia, and English-speaking urban populations in South/Southeast Asia — highest CPM in US/UK/CA/AU specifically |
| **Interests** | Fitness-adjacent (not hardcore fitness), nutrition, productivity, parenting, aging parents' health, sleep optimization, mental health awareness |
| **Pain points** | "I don't have time to research this properly," "I don't know what's actually true vs. marketing," "I feel tired/foggy and don't know why," "I want to prevent problems, not just treat them" |
| **Emotional triggers (ethical)** | Relief ("oh, that's normal"), curiosity ("wait, why does that happen"), quiet self-improvement (not guilt-based), family care ("I should tell my mum this") |
| **Most shareable health topics** | Content that a reader can act on *today* in under 2 minutes, content that corrects a widely-believed myth, content relevant to a family member ("this explains Dad's blood pressure") |

**What NOT to target:** fear-based "you're doing this wrong and it's dangerous" framing. It gets short-term reach and long-term unfollows/reports — the opposite of the 1M goal, which requires retention over years, not one viral spike.

## 7. Engagement Strategy

**Increase comments:** end every post with a question that's answerable in one word or one number — "how many hours did you sleep?" outperforms "what are your thoughts on sleep hygiene?" every time.

**Increase shares:** content relevant to *someone else the reader knows* ("tag a parent," "send this to the friend who...") outperforms content relevant only to the reader.

**Increase saves:** infographics and myth-vs-fact cards are the save-magnets — they're referenced later, which is exactly what a save is for. Lean into carousel/infographic volume specifically to grow this metric.

**Encourage repeat visitors:** a predictable weekly rhythm (Section 5's themed days) gives people a reason to check back on a specific day, not just algorithmically stumble onto the page again.

**Build an active community:** reply to every comment within the first hour with a follow-up question — this is currently at zero activity to build on, so it's a clean-slate opportunity, not a habit to break.

**Turn followers into advocates:** feature a follower's question or story (with permission) in a post — being featured is one of the strongest loyalty triggers available, and costs nothing to produce.

## 8. Facebook Algorithm Strategy (current ranking signals)

- **Watch time / completion rate** on Reels is the single strongest lever for a page this size — it's the only format Facebook actively distributes beyond existing followers.
- **Meaningful interactions** (comments with replies, DM shares) outrank passive reactions heavily — a "like" is close to the weakest signal available now.
- **Shareability** — content shared privately (Messenger/DM) carries more weight than public shares; specific, personally-relevant content gets DM'd more than generic content.
- **Retention** — Facebook tracks whether someone who saw one post from a page comes back; consistency (Section 5's rhythm) is what builds this over months, not any single post.
- **Session time** — content that keeps someone on Facebook (vs. sending them off-platform) is favored; this is why links belong in the first comment, never the caption, on the rare occasion one's needed.
- **Original content** — reposted/lightly-edited content from elsewhere is actively down-ranked now; every item in the existing content mandate is original by design.
- **Posting consistency** — irregular bursts read as spam-adjacent; the fixed 6/day cadence already in place is correct and shouldn't be exceeded even to "catch up."

## 9. Competitor Analysis

I can't browse live competitor pages from this environment, so rather than inventing specific page names and numbers I can't verify, here's the **framework to run monthly** — about 20 minutes, genuinely useful:

1. Identify 3–5 health pages in a similar or larger follower bracket (English-language, evidence-based positioning specifically — not supplement-selling pages, which run a different playbook).
2. For each: check posting frequency, reel-vs-image ratio, whether they cite sources visibly, and comment-reply behavior.
3. Scroll their last 20 posts and note which 2–3 clearly outperformed the rest — that's usually where their signature format lives.
4. **The gap most evidence-based health pages leave open:** consistent, visible sourcing on *every* post (not just occasionally) and a genuine no-fear-tactics policy. Both are already structural requirements of this page's mandate — that's a real, defensible differentiator, not a marketing claim.
5. **Opportunity:** very few pages in this niche run a disciplined myth-vs-fact series with real citations — that series alone, done consistently, is a plausible path to a recognizable niche identity.

## 10. SEO for Facebook

- **Keyword strategy:** work the actual searched phrasing into the *first line* of captions naturally — "morning routine," "blood pressure symptoms," "how to sleep better" — Facebook's search and recommendation systems weight caption text meaningfully.
- **Hashtag strategy:** 5–8 per post, mixing one broad (#health), 2–3 medium (#hearthealth, #sleephealth), and 2–3 niche (#bloodpressuretips) — already the pattern in use; avoid hashtag stuffing past ~10.
- **Search optimization:** keep the About section keyword-natural ("evidence-based health tips," "daily health education") since it's indexed for Facebook Search.
- **Caption optimization:** front-load the hook in the first 2 lines — Facebook truncates after ~3 lines behind "See more," and that's the make-or-break moment for a tap-through.
- **Image optimization:** file names and alt-text (where the platform exposes it) should describe content plainly ("blood-pressure-myth-fact-card") — minor lever, but free.

## 13. Monetization Strategy (ethical, trust-preserving)

| Method | Fit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Affiliate marketing** | Good fit once trust is established | Only products genuinely relevant and tested — a wrong affiliate pick on a trust-based health page costs disproportionate credibility |
| **Digital products** | Strong fit | A simple sourced "myth-busting checklist" or "sleep reset guide" PDF — low production cost, matches the content identity exactly |
| **Sponsorships** | Viable at 100K+ | Only brands whose claims can be honestly verified against the same sourcing bar as organic content — this page's entire value proposition is trustworthy claims, so a sponsor making unverifiable claims would undermine everything built |
| **Brand collaborations** | Same caveat as sponsorships | Health-adjacent brands (sleep, hydration, fitness gear) fit better than supplement brands, which invite skepticism in this exact audience |
| **Online courses** | Long-term, high-effort | A structured "evidence-based habits" mini-course once the page has a recognizable voice and enough audience data to know what people actually want deeper content on |
| **Email list building** | Should start now, independent of follower count | The one asset not owned by Facebook's algorithm — a simple "get our weekly sourced health digest" signup, linked in bio, is a hedge against platform risk and a monetization channel of its own later |

**Rule that protects all of the above:** never let a monetization post skip the sourcing/disclaimer standard applied to organic content. The moment this page's claims become less rigorous when money's involved, the entire trust asset it's spent months building is gone in one post.

## 14. Weekly Review Process

Every week, without exception:

1. **Pull the Section 12 metrics** for the week just finished.
2. **Identify the single best-performing post** (by shares first, per the algorithm weighting) and the single worst.
3. **Ask why** — was it the topic, the hook, the format, the posting time? Be specific, not "reels do better" (too vague to act on) but "the mechanism-explainer reels do better than the list-format reels."
4. **Feed that answer into next week's content mix** — more of what worked, retire what consistently doesn't after 2–3 fair tries (not after one post — single-post variance is normal).
5. **Re-check the audit table (Section 1)** monthly, not weekly — brand/positioning items move slowly and don't need a weekly re-litigation.

This loop is what turns 450 pre-written content ideas into a page that's actually improving, rather than a page executing a fixed script regardless of what the audience responds to.
