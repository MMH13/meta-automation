## 0. The finding that has to come first

I pulled the real numbers before writing any strategy. Across all 25 posts published since July 18 — reviews, watchlists, hot takes, all well-written and all original — the page received **zero** reactions, comments and shares. Page-level insights return no data at all, which Facebook does when reach is negligible.

**This is not a content-quality problem. It is a distribution problem.**

Static image posts on Facebook are shown almost exclusively to people who already follow the page. With 3 followers, the addressable audience is 3 people. You can publish flawless reviews three times a day for a year and the number will stay at zero, because nothing in the system carries a static post to a stranger.

There is exactly one surface on Facebook that reliably distributes to non-followers: **Reels.** They are pushed into feeds algorithmically, watch-time is the ranking signal, and they are the only realistic path from 3 followers to any meaningful number.

**So the honest version of the 1M plan is:** reels are the growth engine, and everything else in this document — reviews, riddles, quizzes, bios — is retention and monetization content that only starts mattering once reels bring people in. Running the static plan alone will not get there. I'd rather say that now than generate content into a void for months.

**The blocker:** producing reels needs AI video and music generation, which this setup doesn't have (you declined the paid image API earlier, and there's no video/music tooling). I can write every reel script, storyboard, voiceover and AI prompt — that's Step 6 and it's below, complete. Turning them into actual video needs either your manual production (CapCut + AI stills + Meta Sound Collection is genuinely viable) or a paid API decision. **That decision is the single highest-leverage thing on this list.**

## 3. Three reel scripts (Step 6) — ready to produce

### Reel 1 — "The Jaws Accident" (32s)
- **Hook (0–3s):** "The scariest shark in movie history barely appears — because it kept breaking."
- **Storyboard:** (1) dark water surface, slow push in (2) a single fin cutting water (3) a floating barrel (4) an empty ocean horizon (5) text card payoff
- **VO:** "Spielberg had a mechanical shark. In salt water, it failed constantly. So he cut it — and showed you a fin. A barrel. Nothing at all. Your imagination did the rest. Fifty years later, horror directors are still copying a broken prop."
- **On-screen text:** `THE SHARK BROKE` → `SO HE HID IT` → `AND INVENTED MODERN HORROR`
- **Music:** slow tense strings, Meta Sound Collection (pre-cleared for FB/IG)
- **AI image prompts:** "dark ocean surface at dusk, single dorsal fin, cinematic, 35mm, high contrast" · "weathered yellow barrel floating in dark water, moody"
- **Caption:** "The greatest movie monster is the one you never see. 🦈 👇 What's the scariest thing you never actually saw on screen?"
- **CTA:** comment + share

### Reel 2 — "1999 Broke Cinema" (40s)
- **Hook (0–3s):** "Six films came out in one year and all six changed movies forever."
- **Storyboard:** rapid title cards, one per film, escalating music, ending on a question
- **VO:** "The Matrix rewrote action. Fight Club rewired a generation. The Sixth Sense made the twist a genre. Being John Malkovich made weird mainstream. Magnolia went for broke. Blair Witch invented found footage on no money. One year. Nineteen ninety-nine. And the reason we'll never repeat it? Studios still funded mid-budget films for adults."
- **On-screen text:** year stamp `1999` persistent corner, film titles as cards
- **Music:** building electronic, Meta Sound Collection
- **AI prompts:** abstract era-evocative visuals only — **no film stills, no posters**
- **Caption:** "The best year in modern film history. 👇 Which 1999 film holds up best?"

### Reel 3 — "Say the Line Wrong" (25s)
- **Hook (0–3s):** "You've been quoting this movie wrong your whole life."
- **Storyboard:** text-driven, fast cuts, on-screen quiz format
- **VO:** "'Luke, I am your father.' He never says Luke. 'Play it again, Sam.' Nobody says that line in Casablanca. 'Elementary, my dear Watson.' Not in a single original story. Three of the most famous quotes ever — and none of them are real."
- **On-screen text:** each quote struck through, replaced with the actual line
- **Music:** light, playful
- **Caption:** "Three quotes everyone gets wrong. 👇 Which one did you believe?"

All three: under 45s, zero copyrighted footage, AI-generated or original visuals only, Meta Sound Collection audio (safest — Meta owns the license, so Rights Manager won't claim it).

## 5. Comment reply playbook (Step 11)

- **Correct answer:** "That was fast 👏 Which one gave it away — the third emoji?" *(asks a follow-up; doubles thread length)*
- **Wrong answer:** "Close! Same director, wrong film. One more guess?" *(keeps them in, never corrects flatly)*
- **Funny comment:** match the joke, then redirect: "Genuinely better than our version 😄 Okay, your turn — give us one."
- **Criticism:** "Fair — plenty of people disagree with us on this one. What would you have rated it?" *(never defensive; invites the rating)*
- **Recommendation request:** answer specifically, then ask back: "If you liked that, try X. What did you last watch that surprised you?"
- **Spam:** hide, never reply. Don't feed it engagement.

Rule: **every reply ends in a question.** Reply length correlates with thread depth, and thread depth is what Facebook reads as a conversation worth showing to others.
